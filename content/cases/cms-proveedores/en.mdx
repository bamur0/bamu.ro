## Context

Suppliers needed to update their product catalog without depending on anyone else. I led the design and development of a CMS from scratch so those updates would show up in real time.

## My role

I owned the full product cycle: research, user flow, design system, interface design, prototype and user testing.

## Structure before screens

Before any screens I mapped the supplier's full flow and the catalog's product families, along with a spec for every data field. That map shaped how product creation and change history were structured.

## Validity as the core idea

In a catalog of reference standards, a product with expired information is a product that can't be sold. So each product's status isn't just another field: it organizes the whole portal. The catalog splits into expired, expiring soon, awaiting approval and discontinued, and the home screen opens with whatever needs attention.

### Every change leaves a trail

Two parties edit the information: the supplier and the distributor. So nobody has to ask who changed what, every product keeps a history with author, date and time, and the old value next to the new one.

### Adding a product without leaving the catalog

Adding a product opens a form over the catalog, with required fields marked and loading, error and incomplete-form states designed. The supplier never loses their place.

## Every state, in three resolutions

Sign-in and its scenarios (including a session open on another device), terms of use, home, catalog, create and edit, history, loading, errors and empty search. All of it in three resolutions, from 1366 to 2560 px, plus a clickable prototype for testing.
