## Context

One of the company's main suppliers operates from India, 12 hours apart. Every catalog update (prices, pack sizes, information validity, availability) arrived by email and someone entered it into the ERP by hand. Between the time difference and manual entry, a single change could take days to show up.

Meanwhile, sales reps were quoting with outdated information: products that were already available still showed as discontinued.

## The proposal

Instead of speeding up the email back-and-forth, remove it. We designed a portal where the supplier manages their own catalog and every change flows into the ERP automatically, with no middleman and no need for both teams to be online at the same time.

## My role

I led UX/UI design on a two-person team over three months: research, user flow, data mapping, design system, interfaces, prototype and user testing, through to release.

## Structure before screens

Before any screens I mapped the supplier's full flow and the catalog's product families, with a spec for every data field. That map shaped how product creation and change history are structured.

## Validity as the core idea

In a catalog of reference standards, a product with expired information is a product that can't be sold. So each product's status organizes the whole portal: the catalog splits into expired, expiring soon, awaiting approval and discontinued, and the home screen opens with whatever needs attention.

### Every change leaves a trail

Two parties edit the information: the supplier and the distributor. So nobody has to send an email asking who changed what, every product keeps a history with author, date, time and time zone, and the old value next to the new one.

### Adding a product without leaving the catalog

Adding a product opens a form over the catalog, with required fields marked and loading, error and incomplete-form states already designed. The supplier never loses their place.

## User testing

Testing directly with the supplier wasn't possible, so we ran simulated sessions with two internal users playing their role. We measured how long it took them to upload a catalog and where they got lost navigating.

The biggest finding was search. The first version searched within specific filters, but users expected something like Google: type anything and find it across the whole CMS. We redesigned it as a global search over the entire database. The remaining fixes covered navigation, interaction, color and translation, since the interface is used by a team that works in English.

## Every state, in three resolutions

Sign-in and its scenarios (including a session open on another device), terms of use, home, catalog, create and edit, history, loading, errors and empty search. All of it in three resolutions, from 1366 to 2560 px, plus a clickable prototype for testing.

## Outcome

Updates no longer depend on time zones: whatever the supplier changes shows up in the ERP right away. With the catalog current, sales reps quote with valid information and recover sales opportunities, such as products moving from discontinued back to available.

The platform launched with one supplier and is now used by three. It's ready to onboard any new supplier.
