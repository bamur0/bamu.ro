## Context

The company moved its internal sales process from a legacy system to its own ERP. The full flow runs from quote to order processing: quoting, offer adjustments, order pre-processing, purchase-order adjustment validation and processing the customer's order.

Quoting is the first step in that flow. It's where sales reps turn each customer request into a quote with products, prices, terms and lead times.

## The problem

The system was already in use, but it didn't cover every real scenario. When a quote needed something unusual, like changing payment terms, separating controlled products or quoting an item not in the catalog, reps handled it outside the system. Every off-system quote meant no traceability and a risk for the order steps that follow.

## My role

I didn't design the quoting tool from scratch. My job was to find those isolated scenarios and design the improvements that let the system absorb them: stakeholder analysis, requirements, flows, interface, QA and rollout. I also worked on adapting the module to operate in Peru.

## The workspace

Quoting starts with a customer email and a commercial history, not a blank screen. So the tool is a three-panel workspace: customer context with its commercial terms, the original request exactly as it arrived and, on the right, the quote being built. The first two collapse when the rep needs room to work the product list.

## What I improved

### Changing payment terms without leaving the quote

Changing a quote's payment terms used to force a workaround outside the system. I designed the change inside the flow and compared two ways to validate it: a single check at the end or a check on every change, to balance control and speed.

### Separating controlled products

Controlled products follow different rules from everything else. The tool now flags them, shows how many of each type there are and lets reps split the quote instead of building a separate one by hand.

### Search that explains, not just filters

Every result shows availability (available, back order, not for sale or discontinued), validity, lead time and linked quotes. When an item can't be added, the design says why and what to do, instead of just disabling the button.

### What can't be found doesn't block the quote

Items that aren't in the catalog move to a research tab. Reps can send what they have and follow up on the rest inside the same system.

## Outcome

The system is in production, and scenarios that used to be handled outside it now have a path inside it. About 20% of the quotes that used to be handled off-system moved into it, with full traceability through to the order.
