## A workspace, not a form

Quoting doesn't start on a blank screen: it starts with a customer email and a commercial history. So the tool is a three-panel workspace: customer context, the original request exactly as it arrived and, on the right, the quote being built. The first two collapse when the sales rep needs room to work the product list.

### Commercial terms always in view

Whether the customer accepts partial deliveries, who bills, the offer currency and payment terms all change how a quote should be built. Instead of hiding them on another screen, they live in the first panel, next to the contact who made the request.

### One request, several quotes

A single request can produce several quotes, each with its own status: new, saved, sent or with items under research. Tabs at the top switch between them without losing the customer context.

### Search that explains, not just filters

Every result shows availability (available, back order, not for sale or discontinued), validity, lead time and linked quotes. When an item can't be added, the design says why and what to do, instead of just disabling the button.

### Partial or full, with freight at the end

To close, the rep decides whether the quote is partial or full, the delivery type and the freight setup. Items that can't be found move to a research tab instead of blocking the quote.
